Hi Dr. Shi,
I actually thought the reviews were pretty useful, although it seems like they missed what we were trying to say with the tuple space contribution and how that separates what we made from what already exists in the field, and why it is a good contribution.
To me, the feedback falls into two broad categories:
The first is the paper itself. There are a few structural things we can improve like related work, stronger references, clearer presentation of the experiments, fault models, and replacing the ai-generated conceptual diagrams with figures and graphs from the actual implementation and evaluation. I think the older paper on Overleaf already has a few of those sections, so if we decide to revise, it might be a good skeleton or jumping-off point (but we'd have to pretty heavily trim out the parts that are no longer relevant).
The second is how we position the work relative to the current state of the field. One thing I do think the reviewers largely missed was the tuple-space contribution, so I think we should make that much more explicit and motivate it earlier in the paper. Beyond that, I think we need to spend more time comparing against modern IPFS and Swarm itself (since a lot of the experiments use Swarm specifically), make the fault model more explicit (e.g., Byzantine assumptions and the reasoning behind the replication factor of 7), and expand the related work and citations.
One thing I'd also want to verify before a resubmission is whether the provider/data decoupling is still a novel contribution relative to modern IPFS, or whether that has already become standard practice. If it has, I think Garrett's random walk/XOR-distance idea may give us a clearer point of differentiation, or at least strengthen the overall contribution.
I think that if we take the reviews at face value and keep our underlying ideas but restructure and redesign the paper around the comments, we'd probably end up with a much stronger paper.

Thanks,
Nick
