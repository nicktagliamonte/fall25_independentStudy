Review #109A
===========================================================================

Overall merit
-------------
1. Reject

Reviewer expertise
------------------
4. Expert

Paper summary
-------------
This paper introduces vn-IPFS, a decentralized storage architecture that re-engineers IPFS using von Neumann’s principles for building reliable systems from unreliable components. vn-IPFS decouples data from providers through a probabilistic Tuple Space abstraction, enabling scalable search, replication, deduplication, and fault tolerance. By combining DHT-based lookup, N-hop replica distribution, automated failover, and read-repair mechanisms, the system aims to provide high availability and partition tolerance for mission-critical storage.

Comments for authors
--------------------
The paper reads as unfinished work: it lacks an evaluation section with experimental results, and several technical claims remain unsupported by empirical evidence.

The paper would also benefit from a proper related-work section. At present, it does not clearly position the proposed approach with respect to existing work on IPFS, DHT-based storage systems, decentralized file systems, replication strategies, or fault-tolerant distributed storage. Moreover, the bibliography is very limited and relies on only a few references, most of which are relatively old, making it difficult to assess how the proposal relates to the current state of the art.

Review #109B
===========================================================================

Overall merit
-------------
1. Reject

Reviewer expertise
------------------
3. Knowledgeable

Paper summary
-------------
The paper proposes vn-IPFS, a decentralized storage system based on a Distributed Hash Table whose stated core contribution is to replace the <provider-ID, CID> mapping with a <CID, list-of-provider-IDs> mapping.

Comments for authors
--------------------
## Novelty of the contribution

The claimed core innovation (replacing the "conventional" <provider-ID, CID> mapping with a <CID, list-of-providers> indirection) is already the existing architecture of IPFS, which the paper neither cites nor compares against despite its title. The problem raised by the paper (having a tight coupling between the provider and the data) seems to concern very old p2p file-sharing systems (Pastry? Chord? I am not really sure to which system the paper refers to), but modern DHT systems (e.g. IPFS) already solve this issue by only storing in the DHT a pointer to the providers, and let arbitrary nodes host the data. As far as I can see, the motivation of this paper is given in only 2 sentences (end of the first paragraph of the introduction, and start of the second paragraph).

## No formalization or correctness argument

There's no fault model stated anywhere (crash or Byzantine? what synchrony assumptions?), no specification of the problem solved, and no argument for the correctness of the design.

## No actual evaluation

The abstract claims "Experimental results demonstrate that vn-IPFS delivers deterministic performance and high reliability," and the intro promises "Preliminary performance results are included", but Section VII contains no experiments whatsoever.

## Absence of related work

The paper has three references, the most recent from 2002. There is no engagement with IPFS/libp2p itself (despite the title), nor with Filecoin, nor with any classical (e.g. Kademlia/BitTorrent) or more modern (e.g. Ethereum Swarm) DHT-based storage systems. Table I compares against a generic "Legacy DHT" strawman, and the claim that existing DHT systems are unsuitable for mission-critical storage is supported by a 2002 open-questions paper. The CRDT literature, which directly addresses convergence under failures, is not engaged at all.

## Inconsistencies

- The token supply is 50,000,000,000,000 in Section II and 50,000,000,000 in Section VI.

- The tuple is <CID+UID, value> in the introduction but <CID, value> in Section II. Furthermore, the role of UID is never defined in the design.

- The paper alternates between <provider, data> and <providerID, contentID(CID)> to denote the "conventional" binding of DHT systems. Also, what's the point of writing contentID(CID)? CID already means content ID.

## Presentation

The paper is clearly poorly written. In addition to the above:

- Substantial passages are duplicated verbatim in the paper:
    - the start of the first bullet of Section I ("The Core Innovation") and the start of Section II
    - the end of the first bullet of Section I ("The Core Innovation") and the start of Section IV
    - the 4 remaining bullets of Section I and the bullet list of Section III

- The end of the second bullet of Section III (which is a copy-paste of the introduction, see before) ends abruptly.

- Section VI reads as a poorly formatted whitepaper (port numbers, opcodes...) rather than a research paper, it has no explanation of the design.

- The von Neumann's framing is purely cosmetic. Nothing in the paper really justifies the connection with the 1952 lecture: there is no reliability analysis, no failure probability bound, no explanation of why each file must be replicated 7 times. Moreover, the reference itself is given as "Available: VonNeumann1956.pdf", which is not an acceptable citation.

- The paper would benefit from better formatting, e.g. math mode for the formulas (N, log, ...), monospace for tuples, ``...'' quotes instead of "...".

- Typos:
    - Section IV: serliazing -> serializing
    - Section VI: Eclips -> Eclipse
    - Section VIII: chartier gossip protocols

Review #109C
===========================================================================

Overall merit
-------------
1. Reject

Reviewer expertise
------------------
2. Some familiarity

Paper summary
-------------
The article proposes an alternative approach to decentralised storage, starting from the IPFS design.
The authors adopt von Neumann's probabilistic principles in their work and present provider-data decoupling as the main innovation relative to IPFS.

Comments for authors
--------------------
The article does not effectively present an implemented solution, but merely some architectural principles and sketches.

The provider-data decoupling, referring to a <CID+UID, value> as an alternative to <providerID, CID>, does not present a visible difference, since the considered value is meant to include a list of providers. Moreover, the regular IPFS implementation already uses a CID primary indexing (not a provider ID), allowing to query any node for the CID, so it's not very clear where the difference is.

The introduction enumerates a series of reliability principles considered in the solution, but again, these are not detailed beyond a very basic idea, and some are just regular measures already considered in multiple distributed storage solutions: partition tolerance present in Cassandra, DynamoDB, etc; automated routing tables and data replication, and other typical design traits.
Many sections of the paper repeat the ideas from the introduction. Section III in particular is a copy of the enumeration in the introduction.

The paper merely expands the generic introduction with superficial additions, typically presenting common ideas found in existing implementations. Among the few which are slightly more detailed is integrating tokenomics with the decentralised storage. The description is, however, again quite shallow, and this idea of monetising storage systems is again not novel (e.g., see Filecoin and others). The architecture description presents just some sketches, which look more like an early draft than finished work, and the performance evaluation claims some unproven complexity results without being backed by any effective implementation.

I don't think the paper can be accepted for publication in the current state.
